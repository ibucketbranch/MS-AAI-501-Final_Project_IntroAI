## Introduction
Identify an AI-driven problem, perform a hands-on project, and deliver a report and
presentation as a team. GitHub is REQUIRED for version control and to measure
collaboration. A README file in the repo is required. Follow PEP 8 for all Python code.

## Team structure
- Complete the Final Team Project survey by Friday (Day 4) of Week 1.
- Instructor assigns teams of 2-3 members by the end of Week 1.

## Experiment design
- Define a problem using a dataset (public or self-provided) and describe its real-world
  organizational or business application.
- Complexity at least comparable to one of the course assignments.
- Use at least TWO different types of AI/ML algorithms studied in the course
  (e.g., Classification, Clustering, Regression), and include experimental comparison.
  - Options: compare different algorithm types, tune hyperparameters, or combine
    approaches (e.g., Clustering + Classification).
- If the dataset has many attributes, consider feature selection.
- Use summary statistics and visualization to explain findings.

## Proposal (1-2 pages, due end of Module 3)
Begin with a clear, unambiguous statement of the topic. Include:
1. Brief discussion of the problem and algorithms to investigate, and the system to build.
2. Specific related course topics (heuristic search, classification, deep learning, NLP, CV, etc.).
3. Examples of expected system behaviors / problem types the algorithms handle.
4. The issues you expect to focus on.
5. A list of books/papers/articles/resources (APA 7) - this becomes the core of the
   report's reference list.

## Datasets and ideas
Choose your own problem and dataset. Sources include Kaggle and the UCI ML Repository.
Example directions on the page: medical imaging (DDSM / Cancer Imaging Archive), human
activity recognition from smartphone sensors (UCI), gender bias in book reviews, image
classification (notMNIST), UCR time series, Lending Club loan status, credit card fraud,
NYSE prediction/clustering, breast cancer benign/malignant, Game of Thrones survival.

Rules/tips:
- Do NOT use problems already analyzed in the course.
- Dataset must not be small or made up ("small" = fewer than 1000 examples).
- Avoid datasets needing excessive preprocessing (some preprocessing is expected).
- For clean structured datasets (10-20 features): explain what the model learned
  (variable importance), do not just report accuracy; compare against simpler models.

## Groupwork requirements
- A significant portion of the final grade comes from this project.
- Each member must contribute equally; clear, ongoing weekly communication is expected.
- Lack of participation can result in a failing grade for the course.
- If team dynamics break down, contact the instructor promptly; an independent path is
  possible with instructor approval.

## Final presentation (end of Week 7)
- Record a 20-30 minute team presentation; every member presents an equal portion.
- Good audio required. Upload to YouTube/Vimeo; put the video link on the title slide.
- Submit the slides plus the recording link.

## Final paper and code (end of Week 7)
- Report (Word or PDF), ~10 pages excluding appendices, APA 7 style.
- Contents:
  1. Purpose, goals, scope; references to papers the project/algorithms are based on;
     whether you adapted a published algorithm or devised a new one; relation to course
     techniques.
  2. Clear specification of the AI algorithms used, with analysis, evaluation, and
     critique; algorithm comparisons preferably shown graphically.
  3. Appendix listing each participant and their detailed contributions.
- Code: clearly documented, clean, PEP 8. Submit code with the report, or provide a
  GitHub link to a public repo in both the report and Canvas.
- Only ONE team member submits the deliverables.
